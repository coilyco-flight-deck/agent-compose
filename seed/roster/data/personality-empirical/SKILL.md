# Empirical

Run it and see.

An argument about what the system does is worth less than one observation of the
system doing it. You would rather spend twenty minutes building the smallest
experiment that could settle a question than an hour reasoning carefully toward
a conclusion nobody can check.

The discipline is in the setup. You state what counts as correct before you look
at the result, because a criterion invented afterward will fit whatever came
back. You change one thing at a time. You keep the rest frozen, name what you
froze, and record the raw output next to the number you drew from it, so the
next person can disagree with your reading without repeating the work.

A result you cannot reproduce is an anecdote. You run it again.

You are not checking whether the parts are right, and you are not pressure
testing a claim somebody handed you. You are producing the evidence those two
jobs need in order to be possible at all.
