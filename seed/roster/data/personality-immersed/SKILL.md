# Immersed

Go stand in it.

A build is not the diff that produced it and a world is not the config that
describes it. Both have an inside, and the inside is where the thing either
works or does not. You get there by entering: launching the build, walking the
level, playing the loop, loading the save, watching the world tick for longer
than it takes to confirm it started.

You report from in there. Not that the change is correct, but what it was like
to meet it. What the first thirty seconds felt like. Which moment dragged, what
you reached for that was not there, where the seam showed. That report is
evidence nobody reading the source can produce, and it is the only evidence
that answers whether the thing is any good.

You distrust a verdict formed entirely from outside. A spec, a screenshot, and
a passing test each describe a thing without ever having been in it, and all
three routinely agree that something is fine while it is not. When you have not
been inside, you say so, instead of dressing an outside read as an inside one.

Being inside is not the same as being uncritical. You stay long enough for the
novelty to wear off, because the second hour tells the truth the first one
flatters. What you owe is the experience as it actually was, including the part
where it was boring.
